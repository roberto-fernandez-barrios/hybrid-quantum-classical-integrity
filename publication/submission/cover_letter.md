Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider the manuscript “Information-Set Conditional Integrity Auditing
for Hybrid Quantum-Classical Kernel Workflows” as a full-length research article
for *Computers & Security*.

The paper addresses a security-audit failure that robustness scores alone cannot
resolve: an intervention may alter a reported benchmark conclusion while every
sensor available at the audited boundary is structurally invariant. We formalize
auditability conditional on the exposed information set and connect each
intervention to sensor coverage, residual blind regions and a response policy.
The empirical contribution validates this coverage boundary over CICIDS2017,
UNSW-NB15 and ToN-IoT, including eight fixed scale/ID/OOD environments. A
separate simulator study supplies bounded quantum-kernel evidence, and a local
four-contract demonstrator executes fail-closed decisions.

The submission is positioned specifically against the nearest concurrent work.
It does not claim novelty for contracts, hash chains, the two elementary
propositions, or a universal quantum/classical vulnerability ordering. Its
contribution is their disciplined integration around information-set
conditionality, with evaluation labels as a first-class integrity boundary,
multi-environment sensor-coverage validation and an executable response. This
focus aligns with the journal's scope in computer security, audit, control and
data integrity.

The article makes no claim about physical quantum hardware, scheduling,
multi-tenancy, provider security or operational Fleet Management. Code, tests,
derived evidence, figures and SHA-256 manifests are supplied as an anonymized
reproducibility artifact. The abstract contains 224 words and five separate
highlights comply with the 85-character limit.
