Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider the manuscript “Information-Set Conditional Integrity Auditing
for Hybrid Quantum-Classical Kernel Workflows” as a Regular Paper for *IEEE
Transactions on Dependable and Secure Computing*.

The protected asset is the integrity of evidence and conclusions produced by a
hybrid workflow. The paper addresses a basic assurance failure: an intervention
can change a reported conclusion while every sensor available at the audited
boundary is structurally invariant. We therefore condition every auditability
claim on a declared information set and connect interventions, coverage,
residual blind regions, and response. Missing evidence is not interpreted as a
pass; the executable contract abstains or blocks fail-closed.

The contribution is the integrated combination of (i) a formal workflow and
information-set model, (ii) exact blind-region statements and a sensor-coverage
method, (iii) validation over CICIDS2017, UNSW-NB15, and ToN-IoT in eight fixed
ID/OOD environments, (iv) a bounded quantum-specific integrity layer, and (v)
an executable four-contract response. The main expansion contains 11,400
deduplicated model-intervention observations. All 3,600 evaluation-label cases
leave features, scores, and predictions invariant; all 1,800 prior-preserving
cases also leave label marginals invariant. Every positive conclusion impact
is exposed by item-aligned joint-outcome evidence. A separate 165-cell ideal-
simulation gate shows complementary circuit, semantic-kernel, algebraic,
repeated-estimation, and output coverage.

This positioning fits TDSC's interest in foundations, methodologies, monitoring
and measurement, experimental evaluation, software validation, and dependable
and secure emerging technologies. The nearest contemporary quantum-integrity
frameworks emphasize lifecycle threat taxonomies, evidence sufficiency, or
multi-level architecture. Our narrower distinction is an operational and
empirically validated rule for which integrity claims are supportable from the
evidence exposed at a particular boundary.

We do not present the two elementary propositions, hash chains, contracts, or
the ZZ-versus-SVC profile as standalone novelty. We make no claim of quantum
advantage, complete lifecycle security, physical-QPU coverage, calibrated
device-noise coverage, provider authentication, scheduling security,
multi-tenancy protection, or operational Fleet Management. Code, tests, derived
evidence, vector figures, and SHA-256 manifests accompany the manuscript.
