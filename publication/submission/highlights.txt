Auditability is conditioned on the evidence exposed at the workflow boundary and on the trusted references the auditor holds; a statistical or historical reference (level A) is not an authenticated same-batch anchor (levels B, C).
Label corruption creates exact blind regions for feature-based sensors; a trusted aggregate of the same batch closes them for material changes, and item alignment is needed only for identity.
The calibrated rules reproduce the structural blind regions across three datasets and eight fixed environments (a consistency check); the informative quantity is how often those blind regions coincide with material conclusion changes and served decisions.
A conformal family rule has finite-sample level at most alpha under exchangeability; in the executed, non-exchangeable design the observed decision false-alarm rates are 0.048–0.058, against 0.125–0.259 for the union of per-sensor rules; the earlier asymmetric rule had no guarantee.
An adaptive attacker who preserves the feature-cluster fingerprint evades calibrated batch-level sensors while keeping 83–91 % of the conclusion changes.
Stronger provenance closes blind regions at a measured cost: the trusted regime interrupts 629 of 1,200 near-null synthetic controls, mostly statistical holds (544) that the calibrated batch-level policy also pays, plus 85 exact-reference blocks.
Policy P3 (coverage-complete abstaining) fails closed on missing coverage only and guarantees no minimum detection power.
