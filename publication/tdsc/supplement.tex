\section{Purpose and Claim Boundary}

This supplement records the complete formal statements with proofs, the
experimental design, the adversary-model reading rule, the statistical units
and their dependence structure, the validation checks, the full tables of the
preregistered gates of artifacts 1.1.0, 1.2.0 and 1.3.0 (including the
amendment that replaced the calibration rule and the adversarial
cluster-preserving gate), the secondary model-impact profile and the
reproduction mapping behind the main article. The frozen geometry-aligned gate adds one
preregistered geometry-aligned sensitivity analysis relating clean-resample
calibration to same-item intervention scoring. It changes no dataset, model
family, kernel, feature map, hyperparameter search, threat class, formal
theorem or primary structural experiment. It adds no QPU,
calibrated-noise, scheduling, provider-security, multi-tenancy, deployed
runtime-service or operational Fleet Management evidence. The exact claims
remain bounded to data-to-evaluation interventions, ideal-statevector quantum
kernels, controlled binomial shot emulation, offline policy evaluation on
frozen outputs and a local executable contract.

Version 1.3.7 adds only the preregistered corrective closure: overflow-inclusive
shared histogram edges repair the two JSD drift coordinates, and the existing
label interventions are scored under the same clean-resample geometry as the
calibration draws. The datasets, environments, splits, models, seeds, attacks,
strengths, batch sizes, policies and structural statements are unchanged;
all earlier evidence directories remain immutable and the new evidence is
manifested separately.

\section{Formal Core with Proofs}

\subsection{Objects}

A workflow state consists of the primitive (stored) artifacts
$s=(X,P,C,E,\xi,g,f,y)\in\mathcal S$ and of the derived artifacts the
workflow recomputes from them: $\tilde X=P(X)$, the semantic kernel
$K_{\mathrm{sem}}=\Phi(C)$ on a fixed probe set, the finite-shot estimate
$\hat K=\mathrm{est}(K_{\mathrm{sem}},E,\xi)$ with estimation randomness
$\xi$, the observed kernel $K_{\mathrm{obs}}=g(\hat K)$ delivered downstream
after post-processing $g$, the predictions $\hat y=f(\tilde X,K_{\mathrm{obs}})$
indexed by item identity $i=1,\dots,n$, and $R=r(\hat y,y)$. An intervention
$a$ overwrites a declared set of nodes (primitive or derived), keeps every
node that is not a descendant of an overwritten node fixed, and recomputes
the descendants of the overwritten nodes through the workflow with $\xi$
retained; the baseline is $s_0$ and $s_a=a(s_0)$. Classes: $T_y$
(label-only: overwrites $y$; only $R$ is recomputed), $T_y^{\pi}\subset T_y$
(prior-preserving), the feature-side mechanisms (overwrite $\tilde X$ or
$X$; $\hat y$ and $R$ recomputed), including the adaptive cluster-preserving
variants of Gate~A, and, on the quantum branch, circuit-side (overwrite $C$),
estimation-variation (overwrite $\xi$ or $E$) and post-processing (overwrite
$g$ or $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$) interventions. The executable counterpart of
these semantics is \path{src/integrity/workflow_state.py}. The conclusion
is $R(s)$ (balanced accuracy) and $\Delta_R(a)=R(s_0)-R(s_a)$; $a$ is
material at level $\tau$ if $|\Delta_R(a)|\ge\tau$. The limit
$\tau\to0^{+}$ is a structural-sensitivity endpoint (any conclusion change),
not an operational risk threshold; $\tau=0.02$ and $0.05$ are the fixed
larger-effect sensitivity analyses. An information set
$\mathcal I$ is a view $V_{\mathcal I}:\mathcal S\to\mathcal O_{\mathcal I}$;
refinement $\mathcal I\sqsubseteq\mathcal I'$ means
$V_{\mathcal I}=\pi\circ V_{\mathcal I'}$; the join $\mathcal I\vee\mathcal W$
is the pair of views. A reference is stored information about the baseline
that the auditor compares the audited batch with; it is trusted when no
intervention of the class can alter it. We describe references on three
separate axes: provenance (historical, benchmark-protected, or deployed
authenticated), granularity (aggregate or item-aligned), and decision
(statistical or exact). The compact classes are not a one-dimensional trust
scale. Class/Level~A denotes statistically thresholded aggregate comparison
and may use a historical population or a clean version of the same item set.
The executed A sensors use the latter as a benchmark-protected oracle without
item-wise pairing; no deployed authentication mechanism is demonstrated.
Level~B is a deployed-trusted aggregate same-batch reference
$\rho=V_{\mathcal J}(s_0)$ with $V_{\mathcal J}$ permutation-invariant;
Level~C is a deployed-trusted item-aligned same-batch reference with
$V_{\mathcal J}$ item-indexed. Thus same items in a benchmark computation
does not imply a trusted deployed anchor. Every selected batch sensor in the
executed policy belongs to Class A; the sensor-level audit below records the
exact current and reference values.

\subsection{Statements and Proofs}

\textbf{Lemma 1 (structural blindness).} If $V_{\mathcal I}(s_a)=V_{\mathcal I}(s_0)$
then $S(s_a)=S(s_0)$ for every sensor $S$ admissible in $\mathcal I$.

\begin{IEEEproof}
$S(s_a)=S(V_{\mathcal I}(s_a))=S(V_{\mathcal I}(s_0))=S(s_0)$. For a
randomized sensor whose seed is part of the view the identity holds path-wise;
with a fresh seed the two outputs are equal in distribution.
\end{IEEEproof}

\textbf{Definition (blind regions and gaps).}
$B_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal A)=\{a\in\mathcal A: V_{\mathcal I}(a(s_0))=V_{\mathcal I}(s_0)\}$;
$B_{\mathcal I,\mathcal S}(\mathcal A)=\{a: S(a(s_0))=S(s_0)\ \forall S\in\mathcal S\}$;
$G_{\mathcal I}(\tau)=\{a:|\Delta_R(a)|\ge\tau, a\in B_{\mathcal I}\}$ and
$G_{\mathcal I,\mathcal S}(\tau)$ analogously. By Lemma~1,
$B_{\mathcal I}\subseteq B_{\mathcal I,\mathcal S}$ and
$G_{\mathcal I}\subseteq G_{\mathcal I,\mathcal S}$, with equality iff
$\mathcal S$ is baseline-separating on the orbit of $\mathcal A$: it takes a
different value at $a(s_0)$ than at $s_0$ whenever the views differ. Pairwise
separation of intervened states (injectivity on the orbit), which
identification of the intervention would need, is never assumed.

\textbf{Proposition 1 (monotonicity).} If $\mathcal I\sqsubseteq\mathcal I'$
then $B_{\mathcal I'}(\mathcal A)\subseteq B_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal A)$ and
$G_{\mathcal I'}(\tau)\subseteq G_{\mathcal I}(\tau)$.

\begin{IEEEproof}
If $V_{\mathcal I'}(s_a)=V_{\mathcal I'}(s_0)$ then
$V_{\mathcal I}(s_a)=\pi(V_{\mathcal I'}(s_a))=\pi(V_{\mathcal I'}(s_0))=V_{\mathcal I}(s_0)$.
Membership in the gap adds only the materiality condition, which does not
depend on the view.
\end{IEEEproof}

\textbf{Corollary 1.} With $f$ fixed and deterministic:
(a) $T_y\subseteq B_{\IXF}\subseteq B_{\IX}$ and every sensor admissible in
$\IX$ or $\IXF$ is invariant under a label-only change; (b)
$T_y^{\pi}\subseteq B_{\IYm}$ and every sensor admissible in $\IYm$ is
invariant under a prior-preserving change.

\begin{IEEEproof}
(a) $T_y$ fixes the primitives $X$, $P$, $C$, $E$, $\xi$, $g$ and $f$.
Therefore $\tilde X$, $K_{\mathrm{sem}}$, $\hat K$ and $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$
remain fixed by their declared dependencies, and so does
$\hat y=f(\tilde X,K_{\mathrm{obs}})$;
the multiset of pairs $(\tilde x_i,\hat y_i)$ is unchanged. Apply Lemma~1 and
Proposition~1 with $\IX\sqsubseteq\IXF$. (b) $T_y^{\pi}$ preserves the class
histogram by definition.
\end{IEEEproof}

\textbf{Proposition 2 (closure).}
$B_{\mathcal I\vee\mathcal W}(\mathcal A)=B_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal A)\cap B_{\mathcal W}(\mathcal A)$;
a class $\mathcal A\subseteq B_{\mathcal I}$ is completely separable in
$\mathcal I\vee\mathcal W$ iff $V_{\mathcal W}(a(s_0))\ne V_{\mathcal W}(s_0)$
for every $a\in\mathcal A$.

\begin{IEEEproof}
The joint view equals the baseline joint view iff both components do.
Necessity: an $a$ that leaves $V_{\mathcal W}$ unchanged stays in the joint
blind region. Sufficiency: a changed component changes the pair.
\end{IEEEproof}

\textbf{Corollary 2.} With $f$ fixed and deterministic, every view factoring
through $(\tilde X,\hat y,\mathrm{hist}(y))$ leaves every $a\in T_y^{\pi}$
in its blind region. The multiset $\IXFY$ separates $a\in T_y^{\pi}$ unless
$a$ permutes labels only among items with identical $(\tilde x,\hat y)$; an
item-aligned reference $\IXFYs$ separates every non-identity relabeling.

\begin{IEEEproof}
Apply Proposition~2 with $\mathcal W$ the view in question: by Corollary~1
each of the three components is invariant, so their join is. For the
multiset of triples, two states have the same multiset iff a permutation of
items maps one to the other; since $(\tilde x_i,\hat y_i)$ is fixed, such a
permutation can only move labels among items with identical
$(\tilde x,\hat y)$. An item-indexed tuple has no such freedom.
\end{IEEEproof}

\textbf{Proposition 3 (materiality forces aggregate separability).} If
$R=g(M)$ with $M$ the confusion matrix, $a\in T_y$ and $\Delta_R(a)\ne0$, then
$M(s_a)\ne M(s_0)$; hence $a$ is separable in the confusion view and in
$\IXFY$, and $G_{\IXFY}(\tau)\cap T_y=\emptyset$ for $\tau>0$.

\begin{IEEEproof}
$M(s_a)=M(s_0)$ implies $R(s_a)=g(M(s_a))=g(M(s_0))=R(s_0)$. The confusion
matrix is a function of the multiset of triples, so a changed $M$ implies a
changed multiset.
\end{IEEEproof}

\emph{Remark (other metrics).} The proof uses only $R=g(M)$, so the statement
holds for accuracy, precision, recall, $F_1$ and every function of the
binarised confusion matrix, with $M$ as the sufficient aggregate. ROC-AUC is
a function of the multiset of (score, label) pairs and not of $M$: two label
vectors with the same confusion matrix can have different AUC. The same
argument then holds with that multiset as the aggregate: a label-only change
that changes the AUC changes the score-label multiset, a trusted stored copy
of it detects every material change exactly, and item identity again needs an
item-aligned reference. Both facts are checked by enumeration in the tests
of the artifact; no AUC experiment is run.

\textbf{Proposition 4 (separability and anchoring).} (i) For any auditor
whose decision is a function of $V_{\mathcal I}(s)$ (including a seed carried
in the view), $a\in B_{\mathcal I}$ implies
$\mathrm{fire}(s_a)=\mathrm{fire}(s_0)$ pathwise under the same state and
reference construction. This pathwise equality does not in general imply
equality with a false-action rate estimated under a different clean-resampling
or reference construction.
(ii) A reference-anchored auditor with trusted $\rho=V_{\mathcal J}(s_0)$
(level B or C) and rule ``fire iff $d(V_{\mathcal J}(s),\rho)>0$'' has zero
false-alarm probability and detects every $a\notin B_{\mathcal J}$ with
probability one.

\begin{IEEEproof}
(i) is Lemma~1 applied to the decision function. (ii) $d(\rho,\rho)=0$ and
$d(V_{\mathcal J}(s_a),\rho)>0$ iff $V_{\mathcal J}(s_a)\ne\rho$, which is
$a\notin B_{\mathcal J}$ because $\rho$ is unaltered.
\end{IEEEproof}

\textbf{Corollary 3 (which reference certifies which integrity).} Let
$a\in T_y$ with $f$ fixed and deterministic. (a) A trusted exact same-batch
claim reference $R_0=R(s_0)$ with ``fire iff $R(s_a)\ne R_0$'' detects exactly
every conclusion change and suffices for a conclusion-only claim. A trusted
aggregate reference $M_0=M(s_0)$ detects every violation of aggregate integrity
and, by Proposition~3, every material conclusion change. (b) Any
reference that factors through $M$, $\mathrm{hist}(y)$ or $R$ is invariant
under relabelings that permute labels among items with equal predictions
(counterexample C1), which violate item-identity integrity; an item-aligned
reference $y_0$ detects every non-identity relabeling.

\begin{IEEEproof}
(a) Proposition~4(ii) with $\mathcal J$ the conclusion view gives the exact
$R_0$ comparison; applying it with the confusion view gives the exact $M_0$
comparison, and Proposition~3 supplies the material-conclusion consequence.
(b) Swapping $y_i,y_j$ with $\hat y_i=\hat y_j$ moves one item
from cell $(\hat y_i,y_i)$ to $(\hat y_i,y_j)$ and another from
$(\hat y_j,y_j)$ to $(\hat y_j,y_i)$; the moves cancel in $M$, hence in every
function of $M$, and the histogram is unchanged, whereas the item-indexed
tuple differs in positions $i$ and $j$.
\end{IEEEproof}

\textbf{Proposition 5 (union of calibrated sensors versus conformal family
calibration).} (a) Let $E_1,\dots,E_m$ be the null firing events of $m$
sensors with $p_j=\Pr_0[E_j]\le\alpha$. Then
\begin{equation}
\max_j p_j\;\le\;\Pr_0\Big[\bigcup_j E_j\Big]\;\le\;\min\Big(1,\sum_j p_j\Big)\;\le\;\min(1,m\alpha).
\end{equation}
Without assuming that some $p_j=\alpha$ the universal lower bound is
$\max_j p_j$, which can be $0$. If every $p_j=\alpha$, then
$\alpha\le\Pr_0[\bigcup_j E_j]\le\min(1,m\alpha)$: the lower bound is attained
by nested (coincident) events, the upper bound $m\alpha$ (when $m\alpha\le1$) by
mutually disjoint events, and under independence
$\Pr_0[\bigcup_j E_j]=1-\prod_j(1-p_j)=1-(1-\alpha)^m$, strictly between the
two bounds for $m\ge2$ and $0<\alpha<1$.

(b) Let $v_1,\dots,v_n\in\mathbb R^m$ be the sensor vectors of the calibration
draws and $v_{n+1}$ that of the audited batch. Give every member $j$ of the
augmented set the leave-one-out family score
\begin{equation}
\tilde U_j=\max_{s\le m}\frac{\#\{i\ne j: v_{s,i}<v_{s,j}\}}{n},
\end{equation}
computed against the other $n$ members, and define
$p=(1+\#\{i\le n:\tilde U_i\ge\tilde U_{n+1}\})/(n+1)$; the family fires iff
$p\le\alpha$, ties counting against firing. Let $k=\lfloor\alpha(n+1)\rfloor$
and $N_j=\#\{i\le n+1:\tilde U_i\ge\tilde U_j\}$. Then (i) for every
augmented set at most $k$ members satisfy $N_j\le k$; (ii) if the $n+1$
vectors are exchangeable,
\begin{equation}
\Pr_0[\,p\le\alpha\,]\;\le\;\frac{\lfloor\alpha(n+1)\rfloor}{n+1}\;\le\;\alpha,
\end{equation}
with equality in the first inequality when the scores are almost surely
distinct, and with no assumption on ties or continuity; (iii) for a single
sensor without ties the rule coincides with ``fire iff $v_{n+1}$ exceeds the
$\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$-th smallest calibration value''; (iv) the level
is marginal over the joint draw of calibration set and audited batch, audited
batches sharing one calibration set are dependent, and nothing is claimed
when exchangeability fails. For $n=200$, $\alpha=0.05$: $k=10$ and the level
is $10/201=0.0498$.

(c) An asymmetric construction scores calibration draws against the other
$n-1$ calibration draws only, the audited batch against all $n$, and fired
when the audited score exceeded the $\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$-th smallest
calibration score. It lacks the claimed level $\alpha+1/(n+1)$.
For $n=4$, $\alpha=0.2$ and three sensors with cyclic orderings
$s_1: j_1>j_2>j_3>x>y$, $s_2: j_2>j_3>j_1>x>y$, $s_3: j_3>j_1>j_2>x>y$,
the members $j_1,j_2,j_3$ each fire when audited (calibration scores
$0.75,0.75,0.25,0$, threshold $0.75$, audited score $1$), so the firing
probability under exchangeability is $0.60$ against the claimed $0.40$ and
against $\alpha$. On the frozen null draws the rule exceeds $\alpha$ under
exchangeable re-splits (Table~\ref{tab:v137-gate-f}).

\begin{IEEEproof}
(a) The union contains each $E_j$ (lower bound); Boole's inequality gives the
upper bound; nested, disjoint and independent events attain or realise the
stated values, the last by inclusion--exclusion. (b)(i) Suppose $k+1$ members
satisfy $N_j\le k$ and let $j^\ast$ be one with the smallest score among
them; every one of the $k+1$ has $\tilde U\ge\tilde U_{j^\ast}$, so
$N_{j^\ast}\ge k+1$, a contradiction. (ii) The score of member $j$ depends on
$v_j$ and on the multiset of the others, so the score map is
permutation-equivariant and exchangeability of the $v$'s gives
exchangeability of the $\tilde U$'s and of the $N_j$'s; $p\le\alpha$ iff
$N_{n+1}\le k$, and by exchangeability
$\Pr[N_{n+1}\le k]=\mathbb E[\#\{j:N_j\le k\}]/(n+1)\le k/(n+1)$ by (i).
With distinct scores exactly $k$ members satisfy $N_j\le k$; ties merge
ranks and shrink that set. (iii) For $m=1$ and distinct values,
$\tilde U_i\ge\tilde U_{n+1}$ iff $v_i>v_{n+1}$, so
$p=(1+\#\{i:v_i>v_{n+1}\})/(n+1)\le\alpha$ iff $v_{n+1}$ exceeds at least
$n-k+1=\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$ calibration values. (iv) restates what
(ii) proves. (c) is checked by enumeration in the artifact tests, which also
verify (i) over every weak ordering (all tie patterns) of up to seven
elements with one sensor, five with two sensors and four with three sensors,
and over random tied configurations with up to ten sensors.
\end{IEEEproof}

\textbf{Proposition 6 (no local authentication without an uncontrolled
root).} Let a \emph{local verifier} be any map
$\mathrm{acc}:\mathcal O_{\mathcal I}\times\mathrm{Ref}\to\{\text{accept},\text{reject}\}$
of the observed view and a reference bundle, let $\mathcal S_H$ be the honest
family of states and $\rho_H(s)=V_{\mathcal J}(s)$ the reference the honest
process attaches to $s$. Assume (honest completeness)
$\mathrm{acc}(V_{\mathcal I}(s),\rho_H(s))=\text{accept}$ for every
$s\in\mathcal S_H$, and (joint control) the adversarial class can produce
$(V_{\mathcal I}(s'),\rho_H(s'))$ for some $s'\in\mathcal S_H$ with
$s'\not\sim_{\mathcal I}s_0$. Then the verifier accepts the substituted pair,
which is observationally an honest run of $s'$, so no function of that input
establishes authenticity of the served state relative to $s_0$; if
$R(s')\ne R(s_0)$ a material change is served. Conversely, if a component
$\rho^{\star}=V_{\mathcal K}(s_0)$ of the bundle cannot be written by the
class, ``reject iff $V_{\mathcal K}(s)\ne\rho^{\star}$'' rejects every
substitution with $V_{\mathcal K}(s')\ne V_{\mathcal K}(s_0)$.

\begin{IEEEproof}
The substituted input equals the honest input of $s'$; by honest completeness
it is accepted, and acceptance is the same event as for an honest run of
$s'$, so no function of the input distinguishes the two. The converse is
Proposition~4(ii) applied to $\mathcal K$. The statement concerns
authenticity, not consistency: by Proposition~3 a material substitution
changes the joint view, but the change is invisible to a verifier all of
whose evidence the class can rewrite.
\end{IEEEproof}

\textbf{Proposition 7 (the quantum branch as an instance of the lattice).}
Let $\mathcal C$ be the circuit representations, $\Phi:\mathcal C\to\mathcal K$
the semantic kernel map on a fixed probe set, $K_{\mathrm{sem}}=\Phi(C)$,
$\hat K$ the finite-shot estimate, $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ the observed kernel,
$h$ the provenance hash, $A$ the algebraic invariants and $f_K$ the decision
from a kernel. Three intervention classes are distinguished: (a)
circuit-side (overwrites $C$; may change $K_{\mathrm{sem}}$); (b) estimation
variation ($C$ and $K_{\mathrm{sem}}$ fixed; $\hat K$ changes); (c)
post-processing or kernel substitution ($C$, $K_{\mathrm{sem}}$ and $\hat K$
fixed; $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ changes). Assume $h$ collision-free on the circuits
under study. (i) On class (a), $[C]=\Phi^{-1}(\Phi(C))$ is the semantic class
of $C$; approved rewrites map $C$ into $[C]$ without fixing $h(C)$, so $V_h$
refines $V_{K_{\mathrm{sem}}}$ and
$B_h(\mathcal A)\subseteq B_{K_{\mathrm{sem}}}(\mathcal A)$, strictly
whenever $\mathcal A$ contains an approved rewrite. (ii) On class (c),
$V_A=A\circ V_{K_{\mathrm{obs}}}$ and $V_f=f_{\cdot}(\tilde X)\circ V_{K_{\mathrm{obs}}}$,
so $B_{K_{\mathrm{obs}}}\subseteq B_A$ and $B_{K_{\mathrm{obs}}}\subseteq B_f$;
a PSD-preserving substitution $K'\ne K_{\mathrm{obs},0}$ with
$A(K')=A(K_{\mathrm{obs},0})$ lies in $B_A\setminus B_{K_{\mathrm{obs}}}$
and, because $C$ and $K_{\mathrm{sem}}$ are fixed on this class, also in
$B_h\cap B_{K_{\mathrm{sem}}}$: it is closed only by a trusted reference on
$K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ itself, so the trusted semantic probe of $C$ and the
trusted observed-kernel reference are different anchors. A kernel change
that crosses no decision boundary lies in $B_f\setminus B_{K_{\mathrm{obs}}}$.
(iii) On class (b), for an honest finite-shot estimate the acceptance rule
``accept iff $\hat K=K_{\mathrm{sem},0}$'' has false-alarm probability
$1-\Pr[\hat K=K_{\mathrm{sem},0}\mid\text{honest}]$, which depends on the
discrete support of the estimator and may be large or equal to one but is
not universally one; exact equality is therefore not an appropriate
acceptance criterion under finite-shot estimation, $d(\hat K,K_{\mathrm{sem},0})$
must be treated as a level-A statistic with a null from repeated honest
estimation, to which Proposition~5(b) applies with $m=1$ when the repeated
estimates and the audited one are exchangeable, and where no such null is
calibrated no statistical integrity claim is made. No inclusion is claimed
across classes.

\begin{IEEEproof}
(i) If $h(C_1)=h(C_2)$ then $C_1=C_2$ by collision freedom and
$\Phi(C_1)=\Phi(C_2)$; hence $V_h$ refines $V_{K_{\mathrm{sem}}}$ and
Proposition~1 gives the inclusion; an approved rewrite $C'\in[C_0]$ with a
different canonical text has $h(C')\ne h(C_0)$ and $\Phi(C')=\Phi(C_0)$.
(ii) is Proposition~1 applied to the two coarsenings of $V_{K_{\mathrm{obs}}}$;
on class (c) the primitive $C$ is untouched, so $h(C)$ and $\Phi(C)$ are
unchanged by the definition of an intervention (Section~2.1), which gives the
membership in $B_h\cap B_{K_{\mathrm{sem}}}$. The set differences are
witnessed in Table~\ref{tab:qcoverage} (15/15 PSD-preserving substitutions
with silent algebraic sensors and unchanged circuit hash, exposed by the
anchored comparison of the delivered kernel; RZ mutations at 0.02 with zero
output change and firing semantic sensor). (iii) For an entry with fidelity
$p$ estimated from $N$ shots, $N\hat p\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(N,p)$ and
$\Pr[\hat p=p]=\binom{N}{Np}p^{Np}(1-p)^{N-Np}$ when $Np$ is an integer and
$0$ otherwise; for $p=1/2$ and $N=2$ this is $1/2$, so the false-alarm
probability of exact equality is $1/2$, not one, and for $p=1/3$, $N=2$ it is
one. For a kernel of independently estimated entries the probabilities
multiply. The universal statement ``$\Pr[\hat K=K_{\mathrm{sem},0}]=0$ under
non-degenerate shot noise'' is false on the support of the estimator; the
conclusion that exact
equality is not an acceptance criterion and that $d(\hat K,K_{\mathrm{sem},0})$
needs a calibrated null stands, with the rest being the definition of the
batch-level auditor and Proposition~5(b). The arithmetic is checked in
\path{tests/test_workflow_state.py}.
\end{IEEEproof}

\subsection{Counterexamples}

Table~\ref{tab:s-witnesses} restates the witnesses of the main text. C1 is
the swap of two labels between items with equal predictions; C3 between items
with different predictions; C4 uses two compensating flips that move the
same number of items across the diagonal of $M$ in each class; C5 and C6
are feature-side; C7 counts every intervened row whose balanced accuracy
increased; C8 is Proposition~3; W9 and W10 count the label rows that a
trusted aggregate reference of the same batch detects (every material one)
and those that only an item-aligned reference exposes (Corollary~3). The
cluster-preserving drift of Gate~A (C9 in the artifact's formal core) is not

\section{Adversary Model: Reading Rule}

Every executed intervention is assigned to one of four classes: \emph{fault
robustness} (benign or accidental change), \emph{integrity corruption}
(non-adaptive change by a party that does not model the auditor),
\emph{adversarial attack} (targeted change to alter the reported conclusion)
and \emph{adaptive attacker} (targeted change designed with knowledge of the
monitored statistics). The complete per-class record (actor, capability,
access point, knowledge, objective, budget, alterable assets, roots the class
cannot write, observable information set and supported claim) is
\path{manuscript/ADVERSARY_MODEL.md} in the artifact; the main article prints
the summary table. The trusted computing base is: the local runtime and host;
the training data, training routine and fitted model; the reference input
array and preprocessing declaration; the reference circuit and probe kernels;
SHA-256 collision resistance; the item-level label reference only where
$\IXFYs$ is declared; and the code that builds and verifies the envelope.

\section{Exact-Statevector Engine Validation}

The expansion evaluator replaces pairwise compute-uncompute execution with a
mathematically equivalent ideal-statevector calculation: it prepares each
unique state $\lvert\phi(x)\rangle$ once and forms
$K(x,y)=\left|\langle\phi(x)\mid\phi(y)\rangle\right|^2$ by matrix
multiplication, with the same eigenvalue-clipping PSD repair as the frozen
Qiskit reference. Validation has four layers: symmetric and cross-kernel
unit matrices agree with the reference at relative and absolute tolerance
$10^{-10}$; symmetry, unit diagonal, numerical range, cache bounds and cache
reuse are unit-tested; a CICIDS 128/128 end-to-end replay (split/model seed
42, dimension 8, ZZ, paper-core suite) preserves every categorical, discrete
performance, impact and integrity-signal endpoint, with only ROC-AUC and its
derived drops differing by at most $1.221896\times10^{-4}$ (one half-tie
increment for 64 positive and 64 negative examples); and the prespecified
256/256 pilot completes. The 240 jobs of Gate~A add a fifth layer: their
clean rows and matched controls reproduce the frozen expansion rows with a
maximum absolute difference of $0.0$ in thirteen columns over 4,200 rows. The
engine is tagged \texttt{exact\_statevector}; it supplies no finite-shot,
noise-model or hardware evidence.

\section{Executable Contract and Policy Composition}

The prototype evaluates four ordered contracts: input/preprocessing (input
array and preprocessing declaration hashes; mismatch blocks); circuit/kernel
(circuit and kernel provenance, semantic probes, symmetry, diagonal,
eigenvalue and PSD-repair evidence; an equivalent rewrite passes only if
explicitly approved); execution/result (declared execution metadata,
repeated-estimation discrepancy and prediction provenance; approved
stochastic execution is held); evaluation/report (item-level labels bound to
predictions and balanced accuracy recomputed; prior-preserving corruption
blocks even when the marginal is identical). Contracts form an ordered
SHA-256 chain: later modification is detectable, but the chain is not signed
and does not authenticate a provider. The contracts fail closed on their
invariants: all passes yield \texttt{allow}; at least one review and no
failure yields \texttt{hold}; any failure yields \texttt{block}. Six
prespecified scenarios and all six chains pass eight strict acceptance checks
in the frozen artifact. The policy layer composes with these contracts by the
maximum in the lattice; the composition over the six frozen envelopes is
Table~\ref{tab:s-composition}, where an exact violation is derived from the
recorded evidence with the approval flags respected. The policy layer is a
pure function evaluated offline on frozen outputs; nothing in this article is
a deployed runtime service.

\section{Geometry-Aligned Sensitivity}
\label{sec:geometry-sensitivity}

The code audit in
\path{manuscript/METHOD_GEOMETRY_AUDIT_v135.md} found a real but bounded
geometry difference. The null-calibrated batch statistics used a fixed frozen
evaluation reference $E$ and fresh clean current batches $C_k$, whereas
feature interventions used the same-item comparison $E$ versus $T(E)$. This
applies to feature JSD, MMD and KS; score JSD; prediction-rate and prediction
JSD; label marginals; and the joint confusion profile. Exact item-aligned
prediction disagreement, label mismatch and trusted aggregate deltas were
already paired and are not part of this finding.

The follow-up was frozen before results in
\path{manuscript/v135_geometry_aligned_sensitivity_prereg.md}, commit
\texttt{62cec146689e3ff8cdb95027c7a9c7371ef767fd} at
2026-09-09T14:46:14+02:00. For every frozen model cell it retains
$s(E,C_k)$ for calibration and $s(E,B_k)$ for clean-resample evaluation, fixes
$B=B_1$, and evaluates every authorized feature intervention as
$s(E,T(B))$. Thus reference, batch construction and statistical unit are
unchanged between clean and intervention. The 240 deterministic replays cover
all eight environments, three dimensions, five split seeds, two model seeds
and the original model/map membership: 600 model cells and 13,200 observations
over 22 existing identity, near-null, feature-control and adaptive conditions.
No label attack, model selection or new random seed is introduced.

Every replay agrees with its frozen clean row within $10^{-9}$, all labels are
preserved, every expected cell is present and all acceptance checks pass. The
exact same-item identity response $s(B,B)$ is zero for all eleven batch scores
and four explicit paired scores (maximum \GeoIdentityMax{}). The aligned
identity copy $s(E,B)$ instead equals the clean-resample row. Its action rate is
therefore the clean-resample false-action rate, not an identity false-positive
rate: \GeoCleanFamilyIX{} in $\IX$ and \GeoCleanFamilyIXF{} in $\IXF$.

Tables~\ref{tab:v137-decomp-4}--\ref{tab:v137-decomp-5} show that mean shift and scaling remain
almost completely responsive after alignment. Dropout remains much weaker and
strength-dependent. Tiny scaling remains highly responsive; tiny Gaussian is
still well above the clean-resample background but is not near-universal. The
near-null conclusion is consequently mechanism-specific rather than a single
unqualified headline.

The same tables give the prespecified matched Gate~A comparison. Across the
12 mechanism/strength/regime comparisons, the adaptive
response is lower in 25--40 of 40 environment/split cells, equal in the rest,
and higher in none. Adaptive material fractions are 0.262--0.667 at matched
strengths. Material nonresponse ranges from \GeoGateMaterialNonresponseMin{} to
\GeoGateMaterialNonresponseMax{}, so materially
altered adaptive rows remain uncovered in every matched comparison. At the
additional 0.25 and 0.50 strengths, adaptive family response rises to
0.853--0.988 in $\IX/\IXF$; the result is not strength-independent.

Relative to the original $s(E,T(E))$ design, alignment raises the matched
adaptive range from 0.007--0.650 to \GeoGateAdaptiveMatchedMin--\GeoGateAdaptiveMatchedMax{} while leaving controls at
\GeoGateControlMatchedMin--\GeoGateControlMatchedMax{}. The same-item geometry therefore amplified part of the original
contrast. It does not explain away the within-aligned-geometry ordering: the
cluster-preserving variant remains consistently below the matched control
while retaining material changes. The supported conclusion is correspondingly
bounded: calibration controls clean-resample false actions but does not
guarantee coverage against this declared monitor-aware attacker. Cluster
structure, geometry and adaptive knowledge interact; the experiment does not
identify a general causal fraction for any one component. Gate~A is a stress
test against the declared cluster-sensitive fingerprint, not general evasion
of drift detectors; the staged dataset structure contributes to its response,
and generalization beyond this fingerprint and structure remains open.

\section{Frozen Sensor Correction and Label-Geometry Evidence}
\label{sec:v137-closure}

The historical JSD defect is reproducible. Each sample was clipped by its own
quantiles, but finite histogram edges were derived only from the reference.
When the current sample lay wholly outside those edges,
\texttt{numpy.histogram(..., density=True)} divided by zero histogram mass and
returned NaN; the historical family path then mapped that NaN to $-\infty$.
The audit finds 3,374 physical NaNs: 744 direct defective coordinates, 230
derived delta coordinates and 2,400 explicitly non-applicable placeholders.
After lineage deduplication these represent 527 scientific evaluations (196
original, 230 aligned-feature and 101 paired diagnostics). Applicable broken
values occur in feature JSD under mean shift and scaling drift; the score-JSD
helper was vulnerable but has no applicable historical NaN. Every canonical
NaN row already had another firing sensor and both family and union fired, so
no historical decision is silently reinterpreted. The complete pre-correction
audit is \path{manuscript/JSD_DEFECT_AUDIT_v137.md} and the row ledger is
\path{evidence/jsd_correction/jsd_historical_nan_audit.csv}.

The corrected construction uses reference-derived shared edges, extends the
outer bins to $\pm\infty$, forms count PMFs, validates positive total mass and
then evaluates JSD. It neither clips a disjoint current sample into the
reference range nor uses a special-case fallback. Under valid finite inputs no
NaN or infinity reaches calibration; the current path raises on any such
value, while the old NaN-to-$-\infty$ rule remains isolated for release
reproduction. Across 64,560 corrected primary observation rows, 58,074
scope-qualified row identifiers have at least one changed JSD coordinate.
There are 4,432 changed JSD sensor-fire coordinates (4,282 rows), 2,265 changed
family-decision coordinates (1,588 rows) and 2,808 changed union-decision
coordinates (1,254 rows). The detailed ledger retains old/new sensor values,
fires and all four regime decisions.

Table~\ref{tab:v137-label-geometry} closes the label-side statistical
geometry question without changing any structural theorem. The primary
finite-batch interpretation uses $s(E,T_y(B))$, calibration $s(E,C)$ and clean
comparator $s(E,B)$: the existing interventions produce 2,700 material rows,
with 343/2,700 conformal and 1,183/2,700 union responses. The
denominator changes because materiality is recomputed on the preregistered
fresh batch $B$, not because rows were selected. Among all 2,836
aggregate-separable interventions, the aligned responses are 352 (12.41\%)
and 1,204 (42.45\%). All 764 aggregate-blind rows retain the exact paired-clean
sensor vector: their attack-only conformal and union increments are both zero.
This is the structural result; nonresponse among aggregate-separable rows is a
finite-batch statistical miss. The original frozen benchmark geometry
$s(E,T_y(E))$ is retained for release reproduction and comparison: it yields
11/2,617 conformal and 43/2,617 union responses, unchanged by the JSD
correction. It remains structurally valid for blind-region invariance but is
not the preferred statistical-response estimate under clean-resample geometry.
Thus the label-only blind region, Propositions~1--7, the $R_0/M_0/C$ hierarchy
and exact C1 results are unchanged.

The policy consequence in Table~\ref{tab:v137-label-policy} is specific to the
aligned label sensitivity and does not replace the frozen all-mechanism policy
grid. It is nevertheless the primary statistical-response interpretation for
the label path. For aligned label rows, P2 serves 2,506, 2,509,
2,391 and 2,357 of the 2,700 material rows in $\IX$, $\IXF$, $\IYm$ and
$\IXFY$, respectively; the trusted regime serves zero. The corresponding
union counts served are 2,201, 1,983, 2,391 and 1,517. The corrected-original
label counts remain 2,606/2,617 served by $\IXFY$/P2; the all-mechanism primary
headline is separately 4,394/7,008 after the JSD correction. Exact item-aligned
blocking remains 3,600/3,600 label interventions and 2,617/2,617 original
material label rows.

Tables~\ref{tab:v137-decomp-1}--\ref{tab:v137-decomp-6} expose the sensor
decomposition for every frozen mechanism and strength, and
Tables~\ref{tab:v137-without-ks-1}--\ref{tab:v137-without-ks-5} give the
corresponding descriptive ablation. KS rejection is retained in the prespecified primary
family. Its dominance is concentrated where the frozen data contain large
point masses or tight near-duplicate clusters: small shifts disperse those
masses even when other aggregate distances remain modest. The frozen-only
without-KS ablation is descriptive and independently reported. In the core
grid, 3,521/4,361 primary $\IX$ family fires, 4,008/4,462 $\IXF$ fires and
3,959/4,419 $\IXFY$ fires survive recalibration without KS; on Gate~A the
corresponding counts are 5,545/6,401, 6,380/6,835 and 6,251/6,712. By contrast,
only 10/650, 62/603 and 62/576 primary near-null family fires survive in those
three regimes. Removal can also create different fires because the remaining
family is recalibrated; therefore ``survives'' and the without-KS total are
both recorded, and neither replaces the primary.

The confusion-profile L1 presentation in Table~\ref{tab:v137-mde} is an exact
lattice implication of the frozen thresholds, not a power guarantee. At
$n=128$, thresholds 0.203125--0.281250 require 13--19 net confusion-cell count
units to exceed them; at $n=256$, thresholds 0.171875--0.203125 require 23--27.
Individual relabelings may cancel in the aggregate, explaining why some
$r=0.02$, 0.05 and 0.10 cells remain below threshold without implying a
minimum attack intensity. E1 is the only 256-row environment and has non-zero
label response, but batch size is confounded with environment and null
behavior, so this contrast cannot identify a batch-size effect.

\section{Secondary Model-Impact Profile}

The ZZ fidelity kernel has the largest mean conclusion impact in the frozen
Gate-1 ID suite. Relative to SVC-RBF, mean impact ratios are 2.524, 3.094 and
3.323 at dimensions 8, 10 and 12; paired within-split differences are
0.02427 (95\% CI 0.01200 to 0.03654), 0.03289 (0.01998 to 0.04580) and
0.03855 (0.02773 to 0.04937), with all five split clusters positive at every dimension
(ZZ/SVC impacts 0.04019/0.01592, 0.04860/0.01571 and 0.05514/0.01659). Across
the eight expansion environments the mean ZZ-minus-SVC impact is positive in
7/8 environments at dimension 8 and 8/8 at dimensions 10 and 12;
environment-specific intervals exclude zero in 4/8, 7/8 and 5/8; means range
from $-0.00009$ to 0.09382, 0.00107 to 0.09508 and 0.00403 to 0.11585
(Fig.~\ref{fig:forest}). Clean balanced accuracy and impact correlate
positively across expansion cells ($r=0.714$, exploratory). The
preregistered ablations (Fig.~\ref{fig:calibrated}(b)) show that the
direction is stable but the magnitude is conditional: standardizing the
quantum branch shrinks the difference to about a third, matched min--max
scaling leaves it unchanged, and cross-validated tuning of both learners
widens it in CICIDS ID and leaves UNSW temporal OOD unchanged. No pooled
population effect is claimed, the profile is neither a quantum-advantage nor
a vulnerability ordering, and the conditional-auditability result does not
depend on it. Figure~\ref{fig:sensor} shows the raw (uncalibrated) sensor
response fractions by regime that motivated the calibration gates.
